Magnetic shielding for MRI

Passive steel shielding that keeps the magnet's fringe field, and the 5-gauss line, inside safe limits.

Why it matters

Contain the fringe field, free up your floor plan

An MRI magnet's fringe field can reach well beyond the scan room. Magnetic shielding contains it, so the scanner fits into tighter sites and surrounding areas stay usable.

Frank Shields uses passive shielding: high-permeability silicon steel fixed to the walls, floor and ceiling, partly or fully, depending on the site. The steel concentrates the magnetic flux lines and reduces the field beyond the shield.

  • Keeps the 5-gauss (0.5 mT) line, the safety limit for pacemaker wearers, within a safe distance
  • Reduces image quality problems caused by moving metal objects such as lifts and vehicles
  • Lets equipment and corridors sit closer to the magnet
  • Built to the scanner manufacturer's shielding design

Materials

Non-oriented electrical steel, M36 grade or 35JN360, in 0.36 mm sheets laminated to the thickness the design calls for.

Supporting structure

We design, fabricate and install the steel frames, including ceiling-suspended frames, and level the floor so the finish is even.

Low-frequency shielding

We also shield against 50 Hz and 60 Hz fields from transformers and power cables near sensitive equipment.
